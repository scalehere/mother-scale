## Key Takeaways

- **Workflows as "virtual assistants":** Once set up, they respond to trigger events (form submit, appointment book) and execute actions without human involvement. No lead is ever left behind.
- **Conditional logic = human touch in automation:** When a lead responds, the workflow takes a different path than for non-responders. Inactive leads get nurture content; engaged leads get moved to sales. This personalisation at scale is the core value.
- **Timing strategy:** Add delays between actions so messages don't feel robotic. Match normal human follow-up cadence (e.g., wait 1 day after resource download, then follow up; wait 2 more days for reminder).
- **Internal notifications:** Workflows can ping a team on Slack/internal notification when a lead hits a key decision point — keeps the team connected without manual tracking.
- **Best practices:**
  1. Start with a clear objective (what does success look like for this workflow — a booked call? a closed deal? reactivated contact?)
  2. Start simple. Complexity = harder to troubleshoot and optimize. Add complexity incrementally.
- **CRM as proactive vs. passive:** With workflows, the CRM shifts from a static contact database to an active, opportunity-generating system.
