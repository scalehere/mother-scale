# The $1.17M/Year Facebook Ads Strategy for Home Service Businesses

## Abstract

Steve Hunsaker's lighting business did $1.17M in one season primarily via Facebook/Instagram ads in a 45–55 day window. This video focuses less on setup and more on: (1) why most DIY accounts fail (one creative), (2) the right metrics to track, and (3) how to stay patient with long-sales-cycle verticals. The marketing math breakdown is the most actionable piece in this source.

## Key Claims

- **The #1 mistake: going live with one creative.** "Show me an ad account that sucked and I'll show you one that went live with one creative."
- **4–8 creatives minimum** per campaign. Mix of owner videos + still images.
- **Lead form campaigns + dynamic images** is the standard setup.
- **Broad targeting**. Very simple. High-income ZIPs + pin targeting for scaling.
- **Long sales cycle businesses must let campaigns run** — don't kill after 2 weeks with no closed jobs. Windows, pool builds, remodels, permanent lighting can take 1–3 months to close.
- **CPL is a vanity metric** — don't optimize for it alone.

## The Marketing Math That Actually Matters

Three metrics in order of importance:

### 1. Cost Per Lead (CPL) — guiding, not the goal
- Easy to game (messaging campaigns get $3 CPL but terrible quality)
- Use as a sanity check, not a decision metric

### 2. Cost Per Appointment (CPA)
```
Cost per appointment = Total spend ÷ number of booked quotes
```
Example: $2,500 spent → 100 leads → 20 booked → $125 per appointment

### 3. Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) — the real metric
```
CAC = Total spend ÷ number of closed jobs
```
Example: $2,500 spent → 5 closed jobs → $500 CAC

### Full Math Example (6x ROAS)
- Spend: $2,500
- Leads: 100 → CPL = $25
- Booked appointments: 20 → 20% lead-to-appointment rate → CPA = $125
- Closed jobs: 5 → 25% close rate → CAC = $500
- Average ticket: $3,000
- Revenue: $15,000
- **ROAS: 6x**

### LTV Exception
For recurring businesses (pest control, pool service, window cleaning plans), month-1 CAC may exceed first-job revenue. Calculate LTV before declaring a campaign unprofitable.

## Real Account Data (Lighting, February–March)
- Spent ~$1,500 in ~2 weeks
- No closed jobs yet (permanent lighting has no demand in Feb/March)
- Pipeline full of sales appointments
- Expected one $5,000 close → instant 3.5x ROAS from one job
- Lesson: **don't judge a campaign before the sales process completes**

## Still Image vs. Video (Live Test)
- Running 9 videos + 11 still images simultaneously
- Still image set outperforming video set in the test week
- Best still image: phone showing app controlling outdoor lights = $18 CPL
- Best video: $57 CPL but pipeline is filling
- Key behavior: find the winner, make variations, pour gas on fire
