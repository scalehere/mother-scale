# Creative Testing Strategy

How to structure, launch, and iterate on Facebook ad creatives for home service businesses. The single most common failure point: launching with too few creatives and quitting before the algorithm learns.

---

## How Many Creatives to Launch

| Source | Minimum Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Dimsey | 2 (simple split test — only image changes) |
| Claeys | 5–7+ videos |
| Hunsaker | 4–8 (mix of video + still image) |
| Hunsaker (1000 ads tested) | 5–10 minimum |

**Agency recommendation**: 5–8 per campaign launch. Mix: 3–4 owner intro video variations (different settings) + 2–3 still image variations. Add testimonial video when available.

---

## Campaign Structure for Testing

```
Campaign (CBO, $X/day budget)
└── Ad Set (location, no interest targeting)
    ├── Ad 1 — Owner intro, in front of truck
    ├── Ad 2 — Owner intro, at customer job site
    ├── Ad 3 — Owner intro, different background/outfit
    ├── Ad 4 — Still image (before/after)
    ├── Ad 5 — Still image (crew + truck)
    └── Ad 6 — Still image (AI-generated promo)
```

CBO at campaign level → Facebook allocates spend to best performer automatically.

---

## What to Change When Testing

Test **one variable at a time** to know what's driving performance:

**Variation 1 — Same script, different setting**
→ Find out: is it the script or the location?

**Variation 2 — Same setting, different offer**
→ Find out: is it the visual or the offer?

**Variation 3 — Same video, different primary text**
→ Note: copy matters less than creative (Dimsey's rule). But still worth testing.

**Variation 4 — Same copy, different image/thumbnail**
→ Highest impact variable for image ads.

---

## How to Read Results

- **Learning phase**: First 3–7 days. Don't kill ads during this phase.
- **Spending pattern**: Facebook will spread spend across all creatives initially, then concentrate on winners.
- **Winner signal**: One creative starts getting disproportionate spend AND lower CPL.
- **What to do with a winner**: Make small variations of it. Change the background. Swap the offer. Test the same script with a different person on camera.

---

## How Long to Run Before Judging

- Short-sales-cycle verticals (window cleaning, pressure washing, lawn care): 2–5 days to first sale
- Long-sales-cycle verticals (remodeling, pools, solar, lighting, ADU): 4–8 weeks minimum
- **Don't kill a campaign because it hasn't closed deals in 2 weeks on a product with a 6-week sales cycle.**
- Judge by: cost per appointment first, then CAC over time. See [[concepts/campaign-metrics]].

## Finding Winning Angles (Pre-Launch)

**FB Ads Library method** (Spencer Claeys):
1. Go to facebook.com/ads/library
2. Search your vertical (e.g., "HVAC", "gutter cleaning", "pressure washing")
3. Note: what angles are being used? What pain points? What offers?
4. Find ads that have been running for months — these are likely working
5. Don't copy word-for-word. Take the angle/hook to ChatGPT and workshop your version

**Signs of a strong angle in the library**:
- Ad has been running for months without stopping
- The hook names a specific pain point
- It has an offer with urgency
- The creative shows a transformation

---

## The Scaling Pattern

1. Launch 5–8 creatives
2. Let algorithm run 5–7 days
3. Identify 1–2 winning creatives (most spend + lowest CPL)
4. Make 3–5 variations of the winner (different setting, different offer, different background)
5. Add variations to the existing campaign
6. Increase daily budget 10–20% every few days
7. Eventually pause lowest performers to concentrate spend

---

## What Kills Testing

- Launching 1–2 creatives (most common failure)
- Killing ads in the first 48–72 hours (learning phase)
- Judging by CPL alone (ignores close rate, LTV)
- Changing too many variables at once (can't attribute wins)
- Overthinking production quality (iPhone is fine)
