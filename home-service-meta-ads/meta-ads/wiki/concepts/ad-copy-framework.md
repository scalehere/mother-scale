# Ad Copy Framework — Home Services Facebook Ads

Synthesized from multiple sources. The copy in the primary text field of the ad (above/below the video or image). Note: multiple sources emphasize the **image/video does 90% of the work** — copy matters less than most people think. But when it does matter, these are the components that convert.

---

## The Full 7-Step Framework (Jonas Olson)

Best for lead form and image ads. Works across all channels — Facebook, Google, postcards, door hangers.

```
1. HOOK (stop the scroll — first 2–3 seconds)
   Action, transformation, or disruption. Movement in video.
   In copy: short punchy opener.

2. LOCAL CALLOUT
   "Hey [City/Neighborhood] homeowners..."
   "Attention [HOA name] residents..."
   → Makes it feel personally addressed.

3. PAIN POINT
   Name the specific problem they already know they have.
   Match to what your actual current clients deal with in that area.
   "Ants in your kitchen?" / "Is your roof covered in black streaks?"
   / "Gutters overflowing every time it rains?"

4. SOLUTION (brief)
   How you fix it. Don't explain the full process.
   Just enough to establish credibility.

5. WHY IT MATTERS (emotional)
   Safety for kids/pets. Protecting home value. Peace of mind.
   More emotional than technical. Dig into the real reason it matters.

6. SOCIAL PROOF
   5-star review. Best if from someone in the same neighborhood
   dealing with the same issue.
   "★★★★★ 'They showed up on time, fixed it same day, price was fair.' — [Name], [City]"

7. CTA (one action only)
   "Click the button below to call now."
   "Click 'Get Quote' to book your free estimate."
   No multiple options. One action.

PS / PSS (offer + urgency — people drop straight to the PS):
   "PS: Book before [date] and get $X off."
   "PS: We only service your area through Friday."
   "PS: If it comes back, we come back free."
```

## Simplified Structure (Spencer Claeys)

For video ads specifically:
```
Problem OR customer callout → Explain why → Simple offer with a reason → CTA
```
Always give a **reason for the offer** (leftover product, slow season, new market expansion). People respond to "why."

## Dimsey's Simple Copy Template

For personal face ads (image):
```
"Hey everyone, I'm [Name]. I've been [service] in [location] for [X] years,
and a few people have rescheduled, so I've got spots open.
[Service description + what it includes].
I back every job with a 100% satisfaction guarantee — if you're not happy, you won't be charged."

Headline: "Get your [X] done"
Description: "[N]+ 5-star Google reviews" or "[N]+ happy customers"
CTA: Get Quote
```

## Key Principles

**Copy matters less than creative** — [[Daniel Dimsey]] ran nearly identical copy for 2 years. The image drives conversion. Don't obsess over copy at the expense of creative.

**One CTA** — never give multiple calls to action. Decision paralysis kills conversions.

**PS is the second thing people read** — headline first, PS second. Put urgency/offer there.

**Vague discounts can work** — "massive discount" without specifying the amount lets the reader's imagination fill it in. Dimsey says nobody ever asked him to honor it explicitly.

**Satisfaction guarantee** — include in every ad. In many countries legally required anyway. Build trust with elderly/first-time customers especially.

**Specificity beats vague** — "2025 Tucson Reader's Choice Award for Best Window Cleaner" beats "the best in the area." Real credentials compound.

---

## Offer Construction

A good offer has:
1. Specific service (AC tune-up, not "HVAC services")
2. Specific price or discount amount
3. Deadline or reason for urgency
4. A reason the offer exists ("leftover product," "slow season," "spots opened up")
