# Cost Per Lead (CPL) Benchmarks — Home Services

Industry CPL benchmarks for home service contractors across Google Ads and Facebook/Meta Ads, by vertical. Source: [[WatsonCo Marketing]] via [[sources/do-facebook-ads-work-home-service-companies]].

## CPL by Vertical

| Vertical | Facebook CPL | Google Ads CPL |
|---|---|---|
| HVAC | $40–$70 | $35–$60 |
| Plumbing | $35–$60 | $30–$55 |
| Electrical | $30–$55 | $25–$50 |
| Landscaping | $25–$45 | $20–$40 |
| Tree Service | $30–$55 | $35–$85 |

**Notable:** Tree service is the only vertical where Facebook CPL ($30–$55) is consistently lower than Google ($35–$85).

## Interpreting CPL

Raw CPL isn't the full picture. Facebook leads carry a lower close rate (10–25%) vs. Google (25–45%). A $45 Facebook lead that closes 15% of the time has a higher effective cost-per-job than a $55 Google lead that closes 35% of the time.

Effective cost per booked job = CPL ÷ close rate

## CPL Is a Vanity Metric (Important Nuance — Added from Hunsaker)

[[Steve Hunsaker]] argues CPL is the least important metric. A $3 CPL from a messaging campaign is useless. A $65 CPL from a friction-heavy lead form may have a better CAC than a $25 CPL with no qualifying questions.

**The metrics that actually matter**: cost per appointment (CPA) and customer acquisition cost (CAC).
