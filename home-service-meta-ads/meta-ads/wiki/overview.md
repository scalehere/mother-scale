# Overview

*Evolving synthesis of everything in the wiki. Currently: 8 sources, all focused on Facebook/Meta ads for home service businesses, agency context.*

---

## The Central Framework

Facebook ads for home services work through **interruption + trust building**, not intent capture. The core job of every ad is: stop the scroll, build enough trust to get a form fill, then convert that warm lead before it goes cold.

The formula that has emerged consistently across all 8 sources:

```
Right creative → Right audience (local, tight geography) → Lead form with qualifying questions
→ Instant speed-to-lead (5–15 min) → Appointment booked → Job closed
```

Every failure point in this chain kills the campaign even if the ads are working.

## What Actually Works (High Confidence — Confirmed by 3+ Sources)

### Creative
- **Owner intro video is the #1 format** (confirmed by Dimsey, Hunsaker ×3, Claeys). 30–60 seconds. Call out neighborhood → name → differentiators → expiring offer → CTA.
- **Branded uniform + truck** in video is non-negotiable for trust building.
- **Before/after still image** is the best image format (confirmed by WatsonCo, Jonas Olson, Hunsaker).
- **Customer testimonial interview** is underused by 99% of competitors — big opportunity.
- **Subtitles are required** on all videos (~40% muted views).
- **5–8 creatives minimum** per campaign launch. 1–2 = failure.

### Targeting
- **Location only, no interest targeting** — creative finds the audience.
- **Tight geography** — city-only or small radius. Exposure effect requires concentration.
- **Uncheck "reach more people"** — prevents waste outside service area.
- **Campaign Budget Optimization (CBO)** — let Facebook allocate across creatives.

### Lead Form
- **Instant forms, More Volume type** with qualifying questions.
- **Contact fields**: name, phone, city. Full address = CPL spike, use only when necessary.
- **Thank-you screen with click-to-call** converts aggressive buyers immediately.
- **Minimum price question** for high-ticket verticals ($1K+) — cuts tire kickers.

### Metrics
- **CPL is a vanity metric.** Track: cost per appointment → CAC → ROAS.
- **Minimum viable math**: leads → booked appts (target 20% rate) → closed jobs → CAC vs. ticket.
- **Long sales cycle**: Don't kill campaigns on week 2 for remodels, pools, solar, lighting.

### Systems (Off-Platform — Equally Critical)
- Speed-to-lead: 5–15 minutes or lead is dead.
- Same-day/next-day quote turnaround.
- Automated follow-up sequences for non-responders.
- CRM for tracking lead → appointment → closed job pipeline.

## Emerging Themes

**Theme 1: Creative is the algorithm's targeting.**
Facebook's algorithm has matured. Interest/demographic targeting adds friction without value. The creative itself self-selects the right audience — a video about HVAC tune-ups reaches homeowners who care about HVAC. Repeated across Dimsey, Claeys, Hunsaker, Jonas.

**Theme 2: Volume of creative testing is the differentiator.**
Most competitors launch 1–2 ads and quit. The winning agency strategy is 5–10 variations, let the algorithm find the winner, then make variations of the winner. This is the actual moat.

**Theme 3: Trust compounds in small geographies.**
Running focused spend in a tight area creates the "exposure effect" — the business owner becomes a recognized local face. This brand effect compounds over time, reducing CAC as frequency builds.

**Theme 4: CPL is a trap metric for agencies.**
Clients (and junior marketers) fixate on CPL. The right conversation is: cost per appointment, close rate, CAC, ROAS. An agency that can speak this language looks smarter and retains clients longer.

**Theme 5: Systems determine outcomes more than ads.**
Multiple sources explicitly state: if you don't have speed-to-lead and follow-up, great ads don't matter. For agency clients, this means either providing or requiring CRM + automation as part of onboarding.

## Contradictions & Tensions

**Image vs. Video**: Dimsey strongly prefers images (simpler, faster). Claeys strongly prefers video. Hunsaker says both — test them together. **Resolution**: No universal answer. Test both per campaign. Hunsaker's live data shows still image sometimes outperforms video week-to-week.

**More Volume vs. Higher Intent form type**: Dimsey uses More Volume and says Higher Intent is worse. Hunsaker also uses More Volume. **Resolution**: More Volume is the consensus. Higher Intent adds friction (slide to submit) that hurts conversions with elderly users.

**Campaign objective — Leads vs. Engagement/Messaging**: Hunsaker and Dimsey use Leads (instant forms). Claeys uses engagement/messaging. **Resolution**: Lead forms are better for agency scale — trackable, predictable, CRM-compatible. Messaging requires someone available to respond to chats.

**Targeting granularity**: Dimsey uses exclusion pins. Hunsaker uses city-only. **Resolution**: Both achieve tight geography. Exclusion pins are more precise; city-only is simpler. City-only for most campaigns, exclusion pins in premium-density markets.
