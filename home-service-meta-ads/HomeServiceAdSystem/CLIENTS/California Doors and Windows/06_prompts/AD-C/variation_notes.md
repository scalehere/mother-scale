## Hypothesis — which background mood predicted to win in market and why

**Predicted winner: V1 (warm SoCal home exterior) — narrowly above V2 (warm home interior). V3 (cool blue Milele control) predicted to underperform both.**

The hypothesis: **warm anchors will outperform the aspirational / luxury anchor for SoCal homeowner conversion on a window-replacement offer.** The test learns whether home-improvement intent (V1), lifestyle / comfort intent (V2), or aspirational / luxury appeal (V3) moves the offer most.

**V1 — warm SoCal home exterior (home-improvement intent anchor).** The buyer scanning the ad sees a soft-focused warm SoCal home (beige stucco, tile roof, late-morning light) sitting BEHIND the offer numeric and the row of 3 windows. The visual reads: "this is the house your install belongs in." The home-improvement frame reduces the cognitive distance between the offer and the buyer's actual house — they aren't being shown a fantasy aspiration, they're being shown a building that looks like theirs. Predicted to convert hardest on Burned-Once Bob and Pacific Beach Pat — both archetypes anchor on "this is real, this is my house, this is the work I need."

**V2 — warm home interior with natural light (lifestyle / comfort intent anchor).** The buyer sees a soft-focused warm interior (sunlight streaming through clean windows, hardwood floor, plant or warm wood furniture). The visual reads: "this is how your home will feel." Lifestyle framing pulls a different mental anchor — the value isn't the install, it's the daily experience of living with the install. Predicted to convert second-strongest, with possible upside on Coronado Karen / Estate Edward warm-retarget segments who are comparing total project lifestyle outcome rather than the install itself.

**V3 — cool blue sky + mountain (Milele-style aspirational / luxury control).** The strict REF-003 background clone — atmospheric, beautiful, but emotionally distant from a SoCal homeowner evaluating a window-replacement contract. The aspirational / luxury frame sells the offer as a status purchase rather than a home-improvement decision. Hypothesis: this anchor underperforms V1 and V2 on cold-prospect conversion because the buyer doesn't see their own home reflected back. V3 serves as the **control variant** — the test reads warm-vs-aspirational cleanly because V3 holds REF-003's exact background.

**Spend prediction within Concept C:** lean V1 at ~40%, V2 at ~35%, V3 at ~25% — run all three in the same ad set so the warm-vs-aspirational comparison is internally consistent. Read after 10–14 days. If V1 and V2 both outperform V3 by a meaningful margin, Round 2 deprecates the strict-clone aesthetic for Concept C and focuses creative budget on warm-mood backgrounds. If V3 outperforms unexpectedly, that's evidence the aspirational frame plays better with the warm-retarget Most Aware audience and Round 2 should split offer-led creative into prospecting (warm anchors) vs. retarget (aspirational anchor) ad sets.

**What this test does NOT measure.** Hero subject (row of 3 windows) is locked — this test does not learn whether a different product cue would convert better. Brand-color hierarchy (yellow halo, red sub-headline, drop shadow, etc.) is locked — this test does not learn whether stronger or weaker brand integration would convert better. Both axes are deferred to a future test pass once the background-mood winner is locked.
