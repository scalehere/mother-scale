Deliberately adding qualification questions to the Meta instant lead form to filter out tire-kickers — even though it raises CPL.

## The trade-off

- No friction: $5–10 CPL, mostly unqualified.
- Friction (e.g. *"Are you aware our minimum is $999?"*): $25–40 CPL, sales reps go to actual buyers.
- The cost-per-appointment can be **the same in both worlds** because lead-to-booked conversion drops by ~10× in the no-friction case. CPL is therefore a [[Marketing Math (CPL → CPA → CAC)]] vanity metric.

## Hunsaker's recipe for [[Valley Premier Lighting]]

1. Multiple-choice: residential or commercial?
2. Multiple-choice: *"Are you aware our minimum pricing for the service starts at $999, but is on a case-by-case basis?"* — required acknowledgement before submission.
3. Contact fields: **first name, last name, email, phone, city** — *not* full address. Asking for address tanks lead volume disproportionately because trust hasn't been built yet.
4. Thank-you screen: "Thanks, you're all set. Feel free to call now for an additional $100 off — just mention this post." Plus a tap-to-call button.

## Pixel cleanliness

Friction's secondary benefit: the Meta pixel learns *who actually qualifies*, not who clicks. As you scale spend, this matters more, not less.
