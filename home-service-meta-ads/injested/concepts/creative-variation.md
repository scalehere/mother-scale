The single most-repeated rule across the corpus: **never publish a campaign with one creative.** Aim for 3–5 minimum, ideally 4–8, sometimes 10–20 for advanced operators.

## "Hunsaker 3:16"

> *"Show me an ad account that sucked and I will show you an ad account that went live with one creative."*

The most common cause of "Facebook ads don't work for my business" is single-creative campaigns at low daily spend. The algorithm needs winners and losers to identify what's working.

## How variation works in the algorithm

- Meta gives each creative a small chunk of the campaign budget for ~24–48 hours during the learning phase.
- The winners get scaled up automatically; the losers get starved.
- Non-winners still serve as **nurture content** — the prospect who didn't convert on creative #1 may convert on creative #3.

## What "variation" actually means

Three modes, in order of operator skill:

1. **Setting variation.** Same script, same value prop — different background. In the truck, in front of vehicle, in warehouse, with crew working behind, with spouse on camera.
2. **Format variation.** Mix [[Owner-Facing Selfie Video Ad]] + [[Testimonial Ad]] + [[Still Image Ads]] in the same campaign for the broadest reach across viewer modes (mute viewers, scrollers, returning prospects).
3. **Hook variation.** Once a winner is identified, vary only the hook (audible vs. visual, neighborhood callout vs. question, urgency vs. social proof) on the winning structure.

## The discipline against ego

- Don't get emotionally attached to creatives. Hunsaker's "Arizona summers are hot" ad was wittier than the winner — and flopped. The algorithm decides; you ship variations.
- "You are not smarter than a trillion-dollar business's algorithm."
