The trap: an owner outsources Meta ads to a $700–$1,500/month agency, the agency under-performs, the owner can't shut it off because the leads they do have come from there, and 50% of advertising budget goes to retainer rather than to the platform.

## The math (cheap-agency case)

- Owner pays $1,000/month retainer + $50/day = $1,500/month → $25/day → **57% of marketing dollars never reach Meta.**
- That same $1,750/month spent entirely on the platform reaches 2× as many prospects.
- "Good" agencies are $2K–$4K/month and only target businesses doing 6 figures/month — their economics support actual daily account management. Cheap agencies' economics force them to onboard at high volume → ~20% monthly churn → entire book turns over in 5 months.

## Why the agency model under-delivers regardless

- The agency makes you film the creative anyway. They write a ChatGPT script, make you record raw footage, and clip it. The owner is doing the highest-value work, paying for the lowest-value work.
- Targeting settings used to be the agency's secret sauce. After [[Creative Beats Targeting]], targeting has been gutted by Meta's algorithm — there's nothing for the agency to "manage" except creative they're outsourcing back to the owner.
- Per-lead pricing is misaligned: agency wants quantity, owner wants quality.

## When agencies still make sense

- Google PPC (always — daily management, expensive keyword bidding).
- Meta only at $1M+/year revenue with a true premium agency ($3–4K/month, retainer + % of spend).
- Hunsaker explicitly: "I'm not anti-agency. I'm anti-terrible agency."

## How to apply

If a home-service operator says they're paying an agency $700–$1,500/month for Meta and the campaigns aren't performing — the answer is to take it in-house, not to switch agencies.
