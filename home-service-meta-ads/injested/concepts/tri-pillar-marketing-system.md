[[Steve Hunsaker]]'s organizing framework for home-service marketing: every customer-acquisition source falls into one of three pillars, and the businesses that scale past the $25–50K/month plateau are running all three in parallel.

## The three pillars

1. **Paid advertising** — Meta ads, Google PPC, Google LSAs.
2. **Organic** — Google Maps SEO, website SEO, referral systems, review nurture, gift-card-driven word of mouth.
3. **Physical marketing** — yard signs, A-frames, vehicle wraps, postcard mailers, branded uniforms.

## Why all three

- Single-channel reliance means feast/famine cycles — when the channel slows, the phone goes silent. Hiring is impossible because work isn't predictable.
- The pillars *reinforce* each other. A paid Facebook lead becomes an installed yard sign that produces a neighbor referral that becomes an organic Google review that lifts ranking that drives more free calls.
- Track via [[Bonfire Analogy]]: brand is the fire pit, organic is the slow-burning log, paid is the gasoline.

## Real evidence

[[Valley Christmas Lights]] across 5 years: paid share dropped 95% → 80% → ~73% → ~60% → ~45% → low 40s as organic compounded — and net margin grew because free calls cost nothing.

## How to apply

When advising any home-service operator: ask which pillar(s) they currently run, and almost always the prescription is to add the missing one rather than turn up the existing one. Owners drowning in work need hiring SOPs, not more ads. Owners with empty calendars need the lead-gen pillars, not "better referral scripts."
