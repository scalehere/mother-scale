The argument: Facebook ads have a **low barrier to entry** (anyone can boost a post) but a **high skill ceiling** (the difference between an A-tier ad operator and the median is 5–10× ROAS). The gap between those is the moat against your local competitors who pay agencies.

## The "high ground" analogy

Hunsaker uses Star Wars Episode 3: Obi-Wan vs. Anakin on the lava planet. Obi-Wan has the high ground — a structural advantage Anakin can't overcome by trying harder. When the owner of a home-service business develops in-house Meta ad skill, they have that same structural advantage over agency-run competitors who are stuck with $1K-retainer slop.

## The skill ladder

1. Boosting a post (grandpa-tier).
2. Throwing AI slop still images into Ads Manager.
3. Owner on camera, unscripted.
4. Owner on camera following [[Six-Step Ad Structure]].
5. Owner video + Fiverr editor with B-roll.
6. Multi-creative testing, [[AI-Generated Creative Workflow]], blended-ROAS reasoning.

Each rung is real local advantage. Home-service competitors are stuck on rungs 1–2 because their agency does the bare minimum.

## How to apply

When evaluating whether to recommend in-house vs. agency Meta ads, the deciding question is whether the operator is willing to climb the skill ladder. If yes, in-house wins on cost *and* quality. If they refuse to film themselves, the channel won't work regardless.
