The bread-and-butter scaffold across every Hunsaker source. Foolproof for a first ad campaign in any home-service trade.

## The six steps (in order)

1. **Local geographic callout.** First words out of the owner's mouth = the city/neighborhood. *"What's up Scottsdale homeowners?"* / *"Do you own a home in Provo, Utah?"* — converts the ad from "stranger pitching" to "neighbor talking." Visual on-screen text can substitute for the audible callout once owners get more advanced (see [[Visual Hook]]).
2. **Owner introduction.** Name + business. *"My name's Todd, I'm the owner of Llewellyn Plumbing."* The face attached to the brand is what humanizes Meta into a trust channel.
3. **What you do.** Concise statement of the service.
4. **Why you're different.** Real differentiators — number of years, awards, technology, family-owned, financing, free design consultation. Avoid generic claims.
5. **Expiring offer / value proposition.** *"$150 off for the next 10 people who respond"* / *"30% off bookings that book in October."* Steve emphasizes that aggressively-explained promos ("we need to fill our October slots before the November rush") often outperform vague discounts because educated buyers reward humanization.
6. **Verbal call to action.** *"Click the get quote now button below."* Not optional — telling them audibly outperforms relying on the visual button.

## Length

30s–~1m30s. Longer gets penalized in placement cost and viewer drop-off.

## Production stack

iPhone or modern Android camera (better than what Hollywood used 30 years ago). $50–$300 lapel mic. $20 tripod. Branded polo + branded vehicle in shot. Subtitles burned in via [[Cap Cut]].

## When to deviate

Never deviate until the structure has produced a winning creative for that account. Then variation is in *setting* (truck, warehouse, customer's driveway, with crew working behind) — same script. Once a setting variant prints, that's when an operator earns the right to test still images, AI ads, and testimonial cuts. See [[Creative Variation]].
