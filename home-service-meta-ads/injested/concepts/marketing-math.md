The metric chain Hunsaker walks through whiteboard-style in [[The $1.17M/Year Strategy]] — channel-agnostic, applies equally to Meta, Google, postcards, yard signs.

## The chain (worked example)

- **Spend:** $2,500 on a campaign.
- **Leads:** 100. **CPL** = $25.
- **Booked appointments:** 20 (20% lead-to-booked rate). **CPA** = $2,500 / 20 = **$125**.
- **Closes:** 5 (25% close rate on appointments). Avg ticket: $3,000.
- **Revenue:** $15,000. **CAC** = $2,500 / 5 = **$500**. **ROAS** = 6×.

## Why CPL is a vanity metric

- Messaging campaigns can produce $3 CPL with garbage leads — anyone clicks the chat icon.
- Removing all [[Lead Form Friction]] crashes CPL by ~80% but the lead-to-booked rate collapses *more*, so CPA stays flat or rises.
- CPL only matters relative to lead-to-booked rate and close rate.

## What actually matters

1. **Cost per booked appointment / cost per given quote.** The first metric where lead quality shows up.
2. **CAC.** The number that lets you compare Meta against Google against postcards.
3. **Avg ticket × close rate** — drives whether CAC is profitable.
4. **LTV** for recurring-revenue businesses (pool service, pest control, window-cleaning service plans). First-month negative ROAS is normal in those models. Month-3 onwards is what counts.

## Front-end profitable

Steve's threshold: $1 in → $3.50–$4 back over the campaign window. ~3.3× ROAS. "Decent" — at that point you keep scaling because every additional dollar still pays for itself, *and* you start harvesting brand mindshare.
