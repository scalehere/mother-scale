[[Steve Hunsaker]] head-to-head comparison (published 2025-12-16). The thesis: Facebook and Google PPC are **opposites**, and ~90% of businesses should run both — but in different stages, with different operational requirements.

## Key Claims

### Facebook Ads
- **Pros:** cheap CPL via in-platform lead forms (no landing page), doubles as a brand builder when you use owner-facing video, easy to scale and pull back, low barrier to entry but a **high skill gap** that can be a moat against agency-using competitors. See [[Skill Gap]].
- **Cons:** lower-quality leads, [[Interruption-Style Marketing]] (prospect isn't searching for you — creative must convince), creative + audience fatigue at scale (frequency creeps as you reach more of the same audience), and **absolutely requires automation** for follow-up — manual calling kills the channel.

### Google Pay-Per-Click (not LSA, not Guaranteed)
- **Pros:** much higher intent (active search), more scalable than Facebook (can keep buying clicks while profitable across more zip codes), less operational lag because lead quality is higher up-front.
- **Cons:** expensive (the most expensive paid platform), extremely competitive with a steep skill gap that **almost always requires an agency** ($2k–$4k/month for a good one), can't start small ($40/day on Google barely registers — owners should put that into Meta instead). Avoid agencies that charge per-lead — incentive is misaligned; prefer retainer or retainer + % of spend.

### Verdict
- Most businesses should run both. Below ~$50K/month revenue, start with Facebook only. Above that, layer in Google PPC via an agency.
- For Steve's businesses: Year 1–2 Google PPC was king; year 3 even; Year 4 Facebook crushed Google in the Christmas-lights business; this year roughly even.
- Owner should **learn Facebook in-house**, **outsource Google PPC** — Google requires daily tinkering; Facebook actively suffers from over-tinkering.
