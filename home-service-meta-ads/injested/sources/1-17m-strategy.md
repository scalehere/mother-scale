[[Steve Hunsaker]] (published 2026-03-16). The most numerically rigorous source in the corpus. Headline result: lighting business did $1.17M last year, ~45–55-day peak season, engine = Facebook + Instagram ads run in-house.

## Key Claims

### Creative + structure
- **Hunsaker 3:16:** "Show me an ad account that sucked and I'll show you an ad account that went live with one creative."
- Standard structure unchanged: location → who/what/why-different → expiring offer → verbal CTA. Logged-up branded uniform required — *no* bacon-stained white tee. "Casualness humanizes you; sloppiness loses you the job."
- Live example: Todd ([[Llewellyn Plumbing]]) Provo water-softener ad — $1,800 spent → ~$30K returned in early days.

### Testing past the blueprint
- Once an account has a winning script, vary background only — same script in the truck, in front of vehicle, in the warehouse, with crew working behind.
- Advanced operators can test outside the formula: AI video, still images, alternate hooks. Steve runs ~20 simultaneous creatives in some campaigns. **Beginners: stay on the blueprint until it's printing.**

### How many creatives
- Floor 4. Ideal 4–8. Mix of video (with subtitles for muted viewing) and still image.

### Patience for the long sales cycle
- Real ad-buying example: $1,500 spent over 2 weeks on a permanent-lighting test campaign in February (off-season). Zero closes yet, but pipeline full. Closing one $5K job converts the campaign from negative to ~3.5× ROAS in a single transaction.
- For long-cycle trades (remodelers, lighting, pool, contractors): view performance in **weeks/months, not days.** Window cleaning / water softening / junk pulls a fast dopamine hit; high-ticket trades require a backfill phone-setter (Lily on dead leads) to crack stale leads later.

### Marketing math (the centerpiece)
- Walks through the chain: $2,500 spent → 100 leads → 20 booked appointments (20% lead-to-booked) → 5 closes → avg ticket $3,000 → $15K revenue. CPL=$25, CPA=$125, **CAC=$500**, ROAS=6×.
- **CPL is a vanity metric.** A messaging campaign can show $3 CPL with garbage leads. The metrics that matter: **cost per booked appointment**, **close rate**, **CAC**, and for recurring-revenue businesses (pool service, pest control, window-cleaning service plans) **LTV** — first-month negative ROAS is normal in those models.
- Same math applies to yard signs, postcards, Google PPC. Channel-agnostic.
