The flagship long-form video from [[Steve Hunsaker]] (published 2026-01-15). Documents his full system across three pillars — paid ads, organic (Google Maps + referrals), and physical marketing — with the [[Bonfire Analogy]] as the conceptual spine: brand is the fire pit, organic is the slow-burning log, paid ads are the gasoline. None of the pillars works alone.

## Key Claims

- Two traps kill mid-stage home service businesses: the **referral trap** (feast/famine, can't hire) and the **agency hamster wheel** (1k–2k/month retainers where ~50% of advertising dollars never reach Meta). Most cheap agencies have ~20% monthly churn.
- The [[Tri-Pillar Marketing System]] is the cure: every paid lead is also an opportunity to plant yard signs, A-frames, and gift-card referrals at the job site so paid spend seeds organic.
- Real share-of-channel data from Steve's lighting business: Year 1 = 95% paid, by Year 5 ≈ 45% paid / 55% organic+word of mouth. Margin compounds as paid % drops.
- Google Maps SEO non-negotiables: a **physical pin/address** (service-area-only businesses lose), **frequency of reviews** (drip them), **photo reviews** (worth more than 4× plain-text reviews), synced socials, owner posts, fully-populated products & services. [[Clifford's Junk Removal]] hit 171 reviews in ~1 year using this exact system.
- Physical marketing non-negotiables: branded yard sign at every job (incentivize with $5 Starbucks card if customers resist), A-frame at the bumper *and* at the neighborhood entrance, gift cards with "gifted by [customer name]" on the back tied to a steakhouse reward when redeemed.
- Sniper-rifle targeting beats shotgun: 50% of his Christmas-light installs come from a 3–4 sq mile radius — concentration multiplies brand exposure, cuts drive time, and clusters referrals. See [[Sniper-Rifle Geographic Targeting]].
- Setup walkthrough mirrors [[How to Run Facebook Ads]]: leads conversion event, $100/day campaign budget, current-city-only locations, **uncheck "reach more people likely to respond"**, leave Advantage+ on for first campaigns, turn off enhancements and multi-advertiser ads, use instant lead forms with friction questions ("are you aware our minimum is $999?").
- Cost-per-lead is a **vanity metric**. The real metrics are cost per booked appointment and customer acquisition cost. See [[Marketing Math (CPL → CPA → CAC)]].
- Implementation roadmap: pick the highest-leverage lever (hiring vs. lead-gen depending on whether you're booked or starving) and finish one system before starting the next — most owners try to do everything at once and end up C-minus on all of it.
