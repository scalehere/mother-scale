# Objection Handling Guide — Marketing Agency for Contractors (San Diego)

Source: `raw/SCALE SD STRATEGY DOCUMENTS/Objection_Handling_Guide_SD_Contractors.docx`

Internal sales reference for handling the most common objections from San Diego contractors. The goal is not to "win" the argument — it's to earn trust and move forward together. Each entry covers the stated objection, the real underlying concern, and a proven response script.

---

## 1. Price & Budget Objections

Price objections almost always signal uncertainty about ROI, not inability to pay. Reframe cost as investment.

### "Your prices are too high."
**Real concern:** "I'm not sure the return will justify the spend."
- Acknowledge: "I totally understand — marketing feels like a big commitment, especially if you've been burned before."
- Reframe: "The question isn't really what this costs. It's what it costs you every month NOT to have a consistent flow of high-quality leads."
- Close: "A single roofing job or kitchen remodel typically pays for six months of our service. What does an average job bring you in gross revenue? Let's figure out together how many new leads you'd need to see a return."

### "I can't afford it right now."
**Real concern:** "I haven't prioritized this, or cash flow is tight."
- Validate: "That makes sense — a lot of our best clients said the exact same thing before they started."
- Pivot: "Most contractors we work with didn't have a 'marketing budget' line item — they created one after seeing results. We also offer flexible month-to-month options."
- Close: "What if we started with a lower-tier package and scaled as your leads come in?"

### "I could just hire someone in-house for less."
**Real concern:** "I think I'm overpaying for something I could do myself."
- Reality check: "A full-time marketing hire in San Diego runs $55,000–$75,000/year before benefits — plus tools. What you get with us is a full team: strategist, ad buyer, SEO specialist, and graphic designer, all specialized in contractor marketing."
- Ask: "What role would you hire first, and how long would it take them to get results?"

## 2. Trust & Credibility Objections

Contractors in San Diego have often been burned by overpromising agencies. Show proof, transparency, and specificity.

### "I've tried marketing agencies before and it didn't work."
**Real concern:** "I don't trust that this time will be different."
- Empathize: "That's one of the most common things I hear — and honestly, a lot of agencies deserve that reputation. Most are generalists who treat a plumber the same as a tech startup."
- Differentiate: "We work exclusively with contractors and home service businesses in San Diego. We know the seasonal demand cycles, the competitive zip codes, and how homeowners in Chula Vista search differently than those in La Jolla."
- Proof: "Can I share a quick case study of a [roofing/HVAC/plumbing] company we worked with here locally?"

### "How do I know you'll actually deliver results?"
**Real concern:** "I need accountability, not just promises."
- Answer directly: "We provide a dashboard you can check any time showing your leads, calls, and cost-per-lead. We don't hide behind 'brand awareness' — you'll see exactly what your money is doing."
- Add: "We also do monthly check-in calls to review performance and adjust strategy. If something isn't working, we tell you — and we fix it."

### "I don't know anything about you."
**Real concern:** "I need social proof before I take a risk."
- Response: "Let me send you links to our Google reviews, a couple of local contractor case studies, and you can talk to one of our current clients directly if you'd like. Transparency is our biggest selling point."

## 3. Timing & Urgency Objections

Timing objections mean the prospect isn't feeling the pain of inaction yet. Make the cost of delay visible.

### "Call me back in a few months."
**Real concern:** "I'm not in enough pain yet, or I'm avoiding the decision."
- Acknowledge then redirect: "What would need to change in the next few months for the timing to feel right?"
- Create urgency: "Your competitors in San Diego are actively running Google Ads and ranking for searches right now. Every month without SEO is a month your domain authority falls further behind. That's time we can't get back."
- Low-risk offer: "What if we at least got the foundation set up — your Google Business profile, local citations, and a quick site audit — while you decide on a full campaign?"

### "It's slow season — I'll wait until spring."
**Real concern:** "Marketing feels like a luxury when revenue is down."
- Reframe: "Slow season is actually the best time to start. SEO takes 3–6 months to show results. If we start now, you'll be dominating search results right when homeowners start calling in the spring."
- Ad angle: "It's also the best time to build your campaign infrastructure without pressure — test, optimize, dial in your messaging so when demand spikes, your budget works hardest."

### "I'm too busy right now."
**Real concern:** "I don't have bandwidth to manage a new vendor relationship."
- Reassure: "Our clients spend an average of 30 minutes per month with us. We handle everything — content, ads, reporting, optimization. We built our process specifically for busy contractors who don't have time to babysit an agency."

## 4. DIY & Competitor Objections

### "I get all my work from referrals. I don't need marketing."
**Real concern:** "My pipeline feels stable — no urgency."
- Reframe: "Referrals are gold — and it tells me you do great work. The challenge is referrals are unpredictable. You can't turn them up when you're slow."
- Ask: "If you could fill your pipeline during your next slow month without waiting for your phone to ring, would that change anything for you?"

### "I'll just do it myself / use AI tools."
**Real concern:** "I don't see the value of paying you when I can DIY."
- Reality check: "Contractors who DIY usually spend 10+ hours/week on it, and results take much longer. What's your time worth per hour on the job? What would 10 hours a week cost you in opportunity?"
- Close: "Our clients hire us specifically because their time is better spent running their crew, not learning Google Ads."

## Key Claims
- Most price objections are really ROI uncertainty — address with math, not discounts
- San Diego contractor market is the specific expertise angle; local knowledge is the key differentiator
- Trust objections require proof: case studies, dashboards, client references — not just words
- Timing objections should be countered with cost-of-delay framing (SEO timeline, competitor activity)
- The 30-minutes-per-month promise is the key to overcoming the "too busy" objection
