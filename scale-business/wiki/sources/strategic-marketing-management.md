A 69-page set of marketing textbook notes covering core strategic marketing theory. Provides the analytical frameworks underlying Scale SD's positioning, targeting, and go-to-market decisions. Key frameworks: G-STIC, market segmentation, customer value mapping, adoption funnel, pricing strategy, and customer equity.

## G-STIC Framework

The master strategic planning structure — five linked components:

- **Goal**: The objective to be achieved (market share, profit, awareness, etc.)
- **Strategy**: The market choice (who to target, what to offer, how to position)
- **Tactics**: The seven action levers (see below)
- **Implementation**: Resources, structure, processes, incentives to execute
- **Controls**: Metrics and feedback loops to evaluate and adjust

Every marketing plan should flow top-down from Goal → Strategy → Tactics and be evaluated bottom-up via Controls. See [[G-STIC Framework]] for full concept page.

## Market Segmentation

Markets are divided by identifiable criteria before a target is chosen:

- **Demographic**: Age, income, occupation, household size
- **Geographic**: Region, urban/rural, climate
- **Psychographic**: Values, lifestyle, personality
- **Behavioral**: Usage rate, loyalty, purchase occasion, benefits sought

After segmenting, evaluate each segment on size, growth, competition, and fit with company capabilities. Select a target and build a positioning strategy around it.

## Positioning

Positioning = how you want to be perceived relative to alternatives in the customer's mind. A strong position is:
- Relevant to the target segment
- Differentiated from competitors
- Credible given what you can actually deliver

The positioning statement format: *For [target], [brand] is the [category] that [differentiating benefit] because [reason to believe].*

## The 7 Marketing Tactics

The operational levers that execute strategy. The classic 4Ps extended:

1. **Product** — features, quality, design, branding
2. **Price** — list price, discounts, payment terms, perceived value
3. **Place (Distribution)** — channels, coverage, logistics
4. **Promotion** — advertising, PR, personal selling, promotions
5. **People** — frontline staff, culture, service quality
6. **Process** — how the service is delivered, customer experience flow
7. **Physical Evidence** — tangible proof of quality (facilities, packaging, reviews)

For service businesses like [[Scale SD / ScaleHere]], People, Process, and Physical Evidence are the differentiating levers — the intangibles that are hardest to copy.

## Customer Adoption Funnel

The staged journey from unaware to loyal buyer:

1. **Awareness** — Does the customer know you exist?
2. **Attractiveness** — Do they want what you offer?
3. **Affordability** — Can they justify the price?
4. **Availability** — Can they access and buy easily?
5. **Loyalty** — Do they return and refer?

Marketing interventions should target the specific stage where customers drop off — not default to "more awareness" every time.

## Pricing Strategy

Two primary approaches:

- **Skim Pricing**: Launch at a high price, capture premium buyers first, lower over time. Best when demand is relatively inelastic and competition is low at launch.
- **Penetration Pricing**: Launch at a low price to capture market share quickly, raise later. Best when scale matters and network effects exist.

For Scale SD's contractor clients: value-based pricing (tying fees to revenue outcomes) reflects skim logic — premium price justified by premium result.

## Customer Equity

The aggregate lifetime value of a company's customer base. Three drivers:

- **Value Equity**: Perceived quality relative to price
- **Brand Equity**: Emotional attachment and associations beyond rational value
- **Retention Equity**: Programs and switching costs that keep customers coming back

The [[Revenue Partner Positioning]] strategy directly targets Value Equity — charging for results, not effort, raises perceived quality and justifies higher retention.

## Key Claims

- The G-STIC framework is the master structure for all strategic marketing plans — goals drive everything downstream
- Segmentation must precede targeting; undifferentiated mass marketing wastes spend
- For service businesses, People/Process/Physical Evidence are stronger differentiators than Product/Price/Place
- The adoption funnel reveals where the real friction is — diagnosis before intervention
- Customer equity is a more useful lens than CLTV alone because it forces focus on brand and retention, not just acquisition
