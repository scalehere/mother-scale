# Scale SD — Ad Voice

> Voice spec for ad scripts and copy. Loaded BEFORE any script writes.
> Subset of `aios/references/voice.md`, scoped to ads.

## The two registers we use

### Register A — static ad headlines, ad copy body, written CTAs
Polished. Direct. Plain English. Benefit-first. Short sentences.

### Register C — spoken video scripts (Peter, Victor, Dani on camera)
Real talk. Working-mind disfluencies preserved. Sounds like a contractor talking to another contractor at a job site, not a marketer reading a pitch deck.

## Hard rules (apply to BOTH registers)

1. **No em dashes.** Ever. Use periods, commas, or new sentences.
2. **No agency jargon.** Forbidden: CRM, automation, funnel, omnichannel, leverage, ROI, KPI, optimize, scalable, holistic, ecosystem.
3. **Contractor language only.** Use the words a contractor would use to a friend at a barbecue. Examples: "your phone's not ringing" not "your lead pipeline is underperforming." "We get you 90 jobs in 12 weeks" not "we drive qualified pipeline at scale."
4. **No claim without verification.** Dollar figures, lead counts, client names — verified in `product.md` proof table only.
5. **No fake guarantees.** Use the locked guarantee verbatim: "90 qualified leads in 12 weeks. If we don't hit it, you don't pay."
6. **Address the contractor directly.** "You" not "contractors" not "owners." Direct address, not third person.

## Spoken script (Register C) structure

Default 3-act: hook → problem → proof → offer → guarantee (if applicable) → CTA.

- **Hook (0-3 sec):** call out audience + create curiosity. Plain English.
- **Problem (3-15 sec):** name the pain in their words. Don't sell yet.
- **Proof (15-30 sec):** point at something real. Job site behind you. Client name. Verified result.
- **Offer (30-45 sec):** what we do. Three things, max. ("We film, we run, we manage. You close.")
- **Guarantee (45-55 sec):** the 90 leads / 12 weeks line.
- **CTA (last 5 sec):** one specific action. "Tap below" or "Link below."

## On-camera delivery rules

- Look at the lens, not the operator.
- Don't smile in the hook. Smile after the hook lands.
- First line is make-or-break. Reshoot if energy drops.
- If a line feels stiff, change the words, keep the meaning.
- Pace: slow on pain, faster on offer, slow again on guarantee.
- Pause is fine. Restart is fine. We cut.

## Static ad (Register A) rules

- Headline: max 7 words. Direct claim or sharp question.
- Body: max 60 words. One pain, one fix, one CTA.
- No list bullets in body copy. Sentences only.
- One concrete number per ad (verified). Either a result or a guarantee, not both.

## Examples — good vs slop

| BAD (slop) | GOOD (ours) |
|---|---|
| "Unlock the power of predictable lead generation." | "Your phone should be ringing every day. If it's not, this is why." |
| "Elevate your contracting business with our holistic marketing solution." | "We film your ads on your job site. We run them. You close." |
| "Drive qualified pipeline at scale with our innovative platform." | "90 jobs in 12 weeks. If we miss, you don't pay." |
| "Are you ready to revolutionize your contractor business?" | "Real talk. Why is your phone slow right now?" |
| "Leverage cutting-edge AI to optimize your lead funnel." | "We get you leads that nobody else has called yet." |

## Spanish voice (Velocity / Rodrigo register)

- Direct-to-camera, contractor Spanish, not academic Spanish.
- Frame technical features in contractor language: "una app que tiene todo" not "un CRM."
- Data-first. Show numbers, screenshots, dollar figures.
- Cadence: faster than English, more confident, less hedging.
- Banned in ES: "solución integral," "optimizar," "potenciar," "ecosistema."

## The 3-strike test (run before publishing)

Read the script out loud. Strike a line if:
1. You hear a word in it you'd never say to a contractor in person.
2. It sounds like an AI wrote it (run it through `writing-rules.md`).
3. It uses a number you can't point to a verified source for.

3 strikes = rewrite from scratch.
