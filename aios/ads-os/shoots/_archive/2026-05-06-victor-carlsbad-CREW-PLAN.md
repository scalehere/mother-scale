# WHAT WE'RE SHOOTING (8 cuts, in this order)

## Cut 1 — Conversation Set A: "Too Busy to Count"
**Length goal in edit:** 15-20s
**Format:** Peter and Victor on site, both visible. Active work behind them.

### How it goes

Peter prompts. Victor answers naturally. Peter pulls receipts.

**Peter:** "Victor, how many leads we sent you last month?"
**Victor:** [downplay, hedge, "honestly I don't know," "a lot," "quite a few"]
**Peter:** [pulls phone] "I'm seeing about {actual number} in our log."
**Victor:** "Yeah, I'd have to look. Been busy."
**Peter (turns to camera):** "See, this guy's too busy with jobs to count the leads. That's a good thing."

### Coaching for Victor
- Don't memorize. Just talk to Peter.
- The hedging IS the proof. Don't try to give a confident number.
- It's fine to say "I don't remember." That's the whole point.

### Coaching for Peter
- Pull the actual number from a real text or our GHL log on your phone. Show your phone screen briefly if natural.
- Don't sound like a salesman closing. You're the receipts-keeper.
- The closing tag is the punchline. Deliver it deadpan to camera, half-smile.

### Run 3 takes minimum.

## Cut 2 — Conversation Set B: "How Long, How Much"
**Length goal in edit:** 20-30s
**Format:** Peter prompts a longer revenue-anchored testimonial.

### How it goes

**Peter:** "How long have you been working with us, Victor?"
**Victor:** [answers natural — 3 months, 4 months, whatever]
**Peter:** "How's the calendar looking now compared to before?"
**Victor:** [natural answer — fuller, picking jobs, etc.]
**Peter:** "And how much new work has come through us, ballpark?"
**Victor:** [hedge, lowball, "a few jobs," "not totally sure"]
**Peter:** [phone glance] "I think it was {figure}. We can pull the breakdown later."
**Victor:** "Could be. Either way I'm not complaining."
**Peter (to camera):** "That's the difference. Ads that put your real work in front of real local homeowners."

### Numbers rule
- Only say a dollar figure on camera if Dani and Ashen can verify it from records tonight.
- If unsure, edit around the number. Keep the conversational vibe.
- "Either way I'm not complaining" is the safety phrase if a number gets dropped in edit.

### Run 3 takes.

## Cut 3 — Conversation Set C: "What Was the Phone Like Before?"
**Length goal in edit:** 15-25s
**Format:** Pain-driven. Sets up the "now" by describing the "before."

### How it goes

**Peter:** "What was the phone like before we started?"
**Victor:** [natural — slow, inconsistent, dependent on referrals]
**Peter:** "What were you doing to fill it?"
**Victor:** [natural — referrals, word of mouth, working long hours]
**Peter:** "And now?"
**Victor:** [natural — picking jobs, calendar fuller, more predictable]
**Peter (to camera):** "That's what it looks like when the phone works. If you want the same thing, link's below."

### Coaching for Victor
- Be specific about the "before." Pain points sell. "I was waking up wondering where the next job was coming from" is gold.
- Don't sell us. Tell the story.

### Run 2 takes.

## Cut 4 — Victor Solo Monologue: "Times Are Changing"
**Length goal in edit:** 15-20s
**Format:** Victor alone, in his truck OR sitting on a finished surface. Peter off-camera.

### Prompt before rolling
Peter says off-camera: "Victor, just tell the camera in your own words why a contractor in 2026 needs marketing. Don't sell us. Just tell the truth from your perspective."

### What we want him to say (in his own words)
- He used to think marketing was for other people / a luxury / unnecessary
- He realized referrals weren't enough anymore
- The phone was slow and he didn't know why
- Once he started running ads, things changed
- He recommends it to other contractors now

### Don't script it.
- Run 4-5 takes minimum, each ~30-45 seconds. Edit will pull the strongest 15-20s line.
- Casual profanity is fine if it's natural to him. Cesar's clients use it. It reads as real.

## Cut 7 — Job Site Walkthrough: Cesar-Style
**Length goal in edit:** 30-45s
**Format:** Peter walks the site, narrates what's being built. Camera follows.

### What Peter does
- Start at the front of the active site. "Hey, we're here in Carlsbad with one of our clients, Victor. Let me show you what's going on today."
- Walk to area A (active work). Describe what's being built. Point at materials.
- Walk to area B (finished work). Show the result. Casual aside about scale or quality.
- Land at Victor briefly. "This is Victor, the GC. He's why this site moves. We do his marketing. He does the work."
- Tag to camera: "If you're a contractor in San Diego and you want your work in front of real local homeowners, link's below."

### Coaching for Peter
- This is unscripted. Just walk and talk.
- Casual profanity is fine if natural ("this thing's huge," "this is sick").
- Don't sell. Show.
- Camera (Dani) follows on gimbal. Smooth movement.

### One full pass + retake the closing tag standalone in case the walk audio is messy.

# WHAT MAKES THIS DIFFERENT FROM SPARK

We watched 13 of Spark's longest-running ads. Here's what we're stealing and what we're improving.

**Stealing:**
- Conversational dialogue format (their longest-runner is 145 days using this)
- Hedging and forgetting numbers (Cesar's clients downplay; we'll do the same)
- Cesar-as-receipts-keeper move (Peter pulls phone for fact-check)
- "Comment 'X' below" CTA pattern
- 5-second teaser with mostly-overlay format

**Improving:**

| Spark does | We do |
|---|---|
| Cesar in truck or office | Peter ON THE ACTIVE JOB SITE (visual wedge they can't replicate) |
| Generic "California" framing | Hyper-local "Carlsbad" / "North County" |
| English only | English today, Spanish track on TikTok next shoot |
| "90 leads guaranteed" | "90 EXCLUSIVE leads, yours alone, never shared" |
| Comment "ADS" | Comment "SCALE" |
| Stock B-roll of trucks | Real B-roll of Victor's actual work |
| Cesar fact-checks from memory | Peter pulls phone to show the actual texts |
| Single-language testimonials | Bilingual planned |
